# Session Recap — Into the Ebonmurk

## A Knight on the Road

After the rather eventful introduction in Duskwatch, Clio, Linley, Needle, and Stillness set out in search of the runaway prison wagon. Somewhere along the road were Linley's confiscated possessions, and the dwarf had no intention of abandoning them.

Their journey soon brought them into the company of an unusual traveler.

He was a knight, splendidly attired and accompanied by a donkey bearing the rather magnificent name of **Invincible**.

The knight introduced himself as **Torvald, Knight of the Flame of Fellowship**.

![TorvaldPortrait.png](/img/user/z_Assets/TorvaldPortrait.png)

Stillness's imposing appearance made an immediate impression upon Invincible, who was considerably less enthusiastic about meeting an enormous silver dragonborn than his master appeared to be.

Torvald, however, proved friendly enough, and upon learning what the travelers were searching for, offered some useful information.

He had recently encountered a wagon moving along the road without anyone controlling it. Stranger still, the unattended carriage had eventually veered away from the main thoroughfare and headed toward the swamplands.

Toward the **Ebonmurk**.

Torvald's demeanor grew considerably more serious as he described the place. Whatever inhabited the swamp, the knight clearly considered it dangerous, and he strongly advised the party against following the wagon into its depths.

Linley was undeterred. At last, he knew where his property had gone, and he wasn't about to let a swamp stand between him and his belongings.

Seeing that his warnings had done little to discourage the travelers, Torvald offered to accompany them. His experience would undoubtedly prove useful, and the party readily accepted his assistance.

Together, they abandoned the safety of the road and ventured into the Ebonmurk.

## Eyes in the Darkness

The Ebonmurk was an unpleasant place.

The firm ground gave way to sodden earth and stagnant pools, while the surrounding vegetation gradually swallowed what little remained of the road. Dark water stretched between the roots of trees, and the deeper they ventured, the harder it became to distinguish movement among the shadows.

It wasn't long before the companions realized they were being watched.

Something moved beyond the edge of their vision. The surrounding swamp seemed alive with faint disturbances, though the darkness concealed whatever creatures were responsible.

Needle decided to investigate.

The little pixie conjured his magical light, casting its glow across the gloomy surroundings.

And the darkness answered with eyes.

![Murk.png](/img/user/z_Assets/Murk.png)

Dozens of them stared back from the undergrowth and shallow pools, reflecting the sudden illumination. What had seemed like an empty stretch of swamp was now revealed to be teeming with creatures that had been observing the travelers all along.

The party had stumbled into a nest of **Murks**.

Not far from where the creatures had gathered, they finally spotted the object of their search.

The prison wagon lay half-submerged in the bog, its wheels trapped in the mire. The horses that had drawn it into this miserable place were nowhere to be seen.

There was little time to investigate what had become of them.

The Murks attacked.

## Thunderwave

The creatures descended upon the party from the surrounding darkness, forcing the adventurers into a frantic melee among the pools and tangled vegetation.

Torvald joined the fighting alongside Stillness and Needle, while Clio sought an opportunity to bring her magic to bear against their numerous attackers.

Unfortunately, the battlefield had become rather crowded.

Murks pressed in around the companions, the stranded wagon lay nearby, and Linley was caught amid the fighting.

Clio decided that the situation called for a spell capable of clearing some space.

She cast **Thunderwave**.

![ClioCastsThunderwave.png](/img/user/z_Assets/ClioCastsThunderwave.png)

A thunderous blast of magical force erupted from the young bard, sweeping outward through the melee with devastating power.

The Murks caught within its reach were battered by the shockwave.

So was the wagon.

And, rather unfortunately, so was Linley.

The dwarf was hurled into the dark swamp, the force of the spell striking him hard enough to render him unconscious.

The wagon fared little better.

Its wooden frame shattered beneath the blast, sending fragments of the carriage and its contents scattering into the surrounding bog. Equipment and belongings that Linley had traveled all this way to recover disappeared into the murky water.

Clio's spell had certainly succeeded in disrupting the enemy.

It had also destroyed the very thing they had come to retrieve.

With Linley incapacitated and the Murks still attacking, there was scarcely an opportunity to assess the damage.

Stillness continued fighting among the creatures, eventually managing to set the wreckage of the wagon ablaze.

What remained of Linley's property was now burning in the middle of a monster-infested swamp.

The dwarf, mercifully, was unconscious for this particular development.

But the party's troubles were far from over.

## The Murk Queen

The creatures continued pressing their attack, and it soon became apparent that the Murks were not merely an assortment of beasts drawn to the stranded carriage.

Something was directing them.

From the darkness emerged a larger, more formidable creature.

**The Murk Queen.**

![MurkQueen.png](/img/user/z_Assets/MurkQueen.png)

The Queen commanded the surrounding Murks, coordinating their attacks against the intruders who had wandered into her domain.

Already struggling against the lesser creatures, the party now faced an enemy capable of organizing their assault. The cramped, treacherous terrain offered little room to maneuver, and with Linley unconscious, the remaining combatants were forced to fight without him.

Stillness, Needle, Clio, and Torvald pressed on against the swarm.

The battle had become a desperate contest of endurance. Every moment spent contending with the Queen gave the lesser Murks another opportunity to attack, and the party had already suffered heavily in the chaos surrounding the wagon.

Yet they refused to yield.

The companions continued their assault, turning their remaining strength against the creature commanding the swamp's inhabitants.

At last, the Murk Queen fell.

With their leader defeated, the remaining Murks were overcome, and the fighting finally came to an end.

The companions stood victorious amid the carnage, battered but alive.

Linley, despite Clio's spectacularly unfortunate application of magic, had survived as well.

The party had won its first major battle.

Although, for the dwarf, victory had come at a rather considerable personal expense.

## The Spoils of Victory

With the swamp finally quiet, the companions examined the remains of their enemies.

Among the Murk Queen's possessions, they discovered a tattered makeshift bundle containing several grisly trophies and twenty gold pieces.

It was hardly a king's ransom, but after their struggle against the swamp's inhabitants, the reward was welcome.

The battle had also provided the adventurers with something considerably more valuable than coin.

They had survived a deadly encounter, discovered the strengths and limitations of their abilities, and earned their first hard-won experience fighting together.

**The party reached Level 2.**

Whatever remained salvageable from the wreckage of Linley's wagon would have to suffice. The companions had little reason to linger in the Ebonmurk, and with their wounded attended to, they began the journey back toward Duskwatch.

## Torvald's Cartography

The return journey offered an opportunity to become better acquainted with their knightly companion.

Torvald had proven himself willing to accompany the party into dangerous territory, and his knowledge of the surrounding region seemed worth taking advantage of.

When the opportunity arose to purchase a map from him, the adventurers accepted.

What they received was certainly a map.

Of sorts.

![Torvald's Map.png](/img/user/z_Assets/Torvald's%20Map.png)

The document was a magnificent display of enthusiastic but deeply questionable cartography, its contents rendered with all the precision one might expect from a child attempting to reconstruct a landscape from memory.

The companions had already noticed the rather crude heraldic designs adorning Torvald's tabard and shield. Comparing those sigils to the artwork before them, it became immediately apparent that all three had been produced by the same hand.

Torvald was evidently a man of many talents.

Drawing was not among them.

Nevertheless, the party now possessed a map of the surrounding region, and the knight's questionable artistic abilities did little to diminish the goodwill he had earned during their adventure.

With the Ebonmurk behind them, they continued toward Duskwatch.

## Back to Duskwatch

By the time the party returned to the Greyfoam Roost, the comforts of a proper tavern were considerably more appealing than another night in the wilderness.

Tallion Briarwisp was once again prepared to provide accommodations, though his hospitality came at its customary price.

Twenty gold pieces for a room.

An amount that, by remarkable coincidence, matched the entirety of the gold they had just recovered from the Murk Queen.

It seemed the proprietor of the Greyfoam Roost was determined to remain the most financially successful participant in their adventures.

Nevertheless, the companions paid for their lodging and finally settled in for some much-needed rest.

Their first expedition had been an eventful one. The Ebonmurk had nearly claimed their lives, Linley had narrowly survived being blasted into unconsciousness by his own companion, and the wagon's recovery had proven considerably more destructive than anyone intended.

But they had returned together, with a little more experience and a new knightly acquaintance.

The following morning, their travels would carry them toward **Dunwich**, where Linley believed a relative of his might be found.

Before setting out, however, the companions had some overdue introductions to make.

It was probably time they learned a little more about the people they had entrusted with their lives.
