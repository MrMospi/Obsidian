# Session 2 — Into the Ebonmurk

## The Missing Wagon

The newly assembled group left Duskwatch behind.

Their objective, such as it was, remained simple.

Find the wagon.

Somewhere ahead of them was the prison wagon that had escaped during the chaos in Duskwatch, carrying the gear and gold Linley was determined to recover.

So Clio, Linley, Needle, and Stillness set out after it.

Four strangers, brought together by a prison break, a missing wagon, and an arrangement that had only recently begun resembling cooperation.

Their journey had barely begun.

And already they were about to encounter someone who knew considerably more about the dangers ahead than they did.

## A Knight on the Road

Along the way, they encountered a knight.

His name was **Torvald**.

More precisely:

**Knight Torvald of the Flame of Fellowship.**

And accompanying the knight was his faithful donkey:

**Invincible.**

![TorvaldPortrait.png](/img/user/z_Assets/TorvaldPortrait.png)

It was a noble name for an animal that, as it turned out, was not particularly comfortable in the presence of enormous silver dragonborn.

Stillness managed to thoroughly frighten the poor creature.

Whatever the donkey thought of their newest acquaintances, Torvald himself proved considerably more accommodating.

More importantly, he possessed something the party desperately needed.

Information.

He had seen the runaway prison wagon traveling along the road without anyone apparently controlling it.

An unattended carriage moving of its own accord was certainly unusual, but the knight seemed considerably more troubled by its destination.

The wagon had veered off the main road and had been heading toward the swamp.

Toward the **Ebonmurk**.

For Linley, this was excellent news. At last, they had a lead.

For Torvald, it was cause for concern.

The knight strongly advised the travelers against pursuing the carriage into the swamp. Whatever lurked within, he evidently considered it dangerous enough that the 

sensible course of action was to turn back.

Unfortunately, the party had already demonstrated a rather questionable relationship with sensible courses of action.

They intended to retrieve Linley's wagon.

And if the wagon had wandered into the Ebonmurk, then the Ebonmurk was where they would go.

Despite his reservations, Torvald offered to accompany them.

Perhaps the knight hoped his presence might keep these strangers from getting themselves killed.

Perhaps he simply recognized that they were going to enter the swamp regardless of what he said.

Whatever his reasoning, the four travelers who had left Duskwatch together were now five.

Together, they continued toward the Ebonmurk.

## The Ebonmurk

The road eventually carried them into the swamp.

Almost immediately, it became apparent that Torvald's warnings had not been entirely unfounded.

The Ebonmurk was not empty.

Far from it.

As the group pushed deeper into the wetlands, they became increasingly aware of movement around them.

Something was out there.

More accurately, several somethings.

They could sense creatures lurking nearby, but the darkness and surrounding swamp made it difficult to determine precisely what was watching them.

Needle decided to remedy that.

The little pixie illuminated the area with his **pixie light**.

And suddenly, the darkness surrounding the party became considerably more revealing.

Eyes.

All around them.

![Murk.png](/img/user/z_Assets/Murk.png)

Pairs of eyes stared back from the gloom, watching the travelers from the surrounding swamp.

Whatever had been moving around them was no longer merely a suspicion.

They were surrounded.

And now the creatures knew the party could see them.

The missing wagon was found half submerged in the bog with the horses that had pulled it to this miserable place no where in sight.

Unfortunately, its recovery was about to become a rather secondary concern.

## Surrounded

The creatures attacked.

Murks.

The swamp's inhabitants descended upon the travelers, turning their search for the missing wagon into a desperate fight.

For a party that had only recently assembled, this was their first real opportunity to discover exactly how well they could fight together.

The answer was complicated.

Clio, eager to assist her new companions, turned to her magic.

The situation called for something powerful enough to drive back the creatures pressing around them.

She chose **Thunderwave**.

![ClioCastsThunderwave.png](/img/user/z_Assets/ClioCastsThunderwave.png)

A tremendous burst of magical force erupted from the young bard, blasting outward into the surrounding melee.

It was an impressive display of destructive magic.

There was, however, one rather unfortunate problem.

**Both the wagon and her new found companions were in the way.**

The spell devastated the nearby wagon, Murks and a certain unprepared dwarf, sending him flying into the dark swamp.

The very wagon they had traveled all this way to recover was shattered by the magnitude of the spell and the belongings it carried were sent flying into the dark of the 

swamp as well.

Clio had successfully demonstrated that she was capable of producing considerable magical force.

She had also demonstrated that she was not yet particularly good at distinguishing between the people she wanted to blast and the people she wanted to keep alive.

For Linley, who had enlisted these strangers to help retrieve his property, this was probably not the outcome he'd envisioned.

And the battle was hardly over.

Stillness fought among the creatures as the others struggled to keep the Murks at bay.

At some point during the fighting, the situation surrounding Linley's missing property deteriorated even further.

**Stillness set the remnants of the wagon on fire.**



It was becoming increasingly apparent that Linley's new companions were going to require a little practice at this whole adventuring business.

Or perhaps it would be if  the dwarf was not unconscious.

His wagon was burning, it's contents scattered into the night.

And the swamp was still full of monsters.

Their first expedition together was going splendidly.

## The Murk Queen

As the battle continued, the party discovered that the creatures surrounding them were not the only danger lurking within the Ebonmurk.

Something larger emerged.

The **Murk Queen**, the creature directing the hordes of Murks and coordinating their attacks.

![MurkQueen.png](/img/user/z_Assets/MurkQueen.png)

Whatever confidence the companions might have gained from holding their own against the lesser Murks was suddenly put to the test.

They now found themselves fighting for their lives against something considerably more dangerous than any of them had anticipated.

Linley had already fallen.

The others were battered.

And the Queen was not about to surrender her territory simply because a collection of strangers had wandered into it.

The party fought on.

Clio's magic, Stillness's strength, Needle's ferocity, and the efforts of their newfound knightly companion were put to the test.

It was a messy battle.

An improvised battle.

A battle fought by people who scarcely knew one another, much less how best to work together.

But somehow, despite the confusion, the friendly fire, and the rather questionable treatment of the wagon they had come to retrieve, they prevailed.

The Murks were defeated.

The Murk Queen fell.

And Linley, despite Clio's rather enthusiastic contribution to his injuries, survived.

The party had won.

## A Hard-Earned Victory

The immediate danger had passed.

The swamp had been survived.

The wagon had been found, although the circumstances of its recovery were considerably more destructive than Linley would likely have preferred.

But the companions had gained something more valuable than missing property.

Experience.

Actual experience.

The sort that came from facing a genuine threat, making mistakes, and discovering that they could somehow emerge victorious despite those mistakes.

There had been close calls.

There had been injuries.

There had been at least one spectacularly unfortunate application of magic.

But they were alive.

On the corpse of the Murk Queen the party discovered a tattered makeshift bundle containing several grizzly trophies and twenty gold pieces .

Their expedition had not been entirely without material reward.

More importantly, the experience had pushed the fledgling adventurers to new heights.

**The party reached Level 2.**

It was a modest achievement in the grand scheme of things.

But for five travelers who had only recently learned one another's names, surviving the Ebonmurk was no small accomplishment.

They had entered the swamp as an unlikely collection of strangers.

They emerged as an unlikely collection of strangers who had now fought a monster together.

Progress.

## Back to Duskwatch

With the Murks defeated and the immediate danger behind them, the party finally turned back toward Duskwatch.

Their first excursion had certainly provided more excitement than anyone could reasonably have expected from a simple search for a missing wagon.

On their journey back to Duskwatch, the party purchased a map off of their new friend Torvald. Or rather, his extremely poor attempt at a map.

It became immediately to anyone looking at this map and the crudely drawn sigils adorning the knight's tabard and shield that they were the products of the same 

person.

![Torvald's Map.png\|500](/img/user/z_Assets/Torvald's%20Map.png)

They returned to the tavern, where the prospect of a roof overhead and a proper bed was suddenly considerably more appealing than another expedition into a monster-

infested swamp.

A room cost them twenty gold pieces.

Not an insignificant sum, particularly considering how recently they had acquired that same amount.

Nevertheless, after the day's events, there was little appetite for arguing over the expense.

The party settled in for the night.

They had survived their first battle.

They had recovered from their first real injuries.

By morning, however, their travels were already preparing to carry them somewhere new.

Their next destination was **Dunwich**, a village far south that Linley presumed was where a relative of his might be.

And this time, before setting out, the people who had spent the last two days escaping guards, chasing wagons, and fighting monsters finally began doing something 

they had somehow neglected to do until now.

They started learning who the hell they were traveling with.
