# Session Recap — Two Days Until the Lantern Festival

## No-Name’s Temporary Refuge

The session resumed beneath Farhaven.

No-Name remained hidden near the sewer entrance currently being used by Fel’Garrak’s excavation team to access Hamyrhal, wounded and displaced from the fungal cavern that had once been his home.

For the moment, the refuge would have to do.

The party reinforced what they could and left behind the makeshift alarm they had constructed for him. They promised they would return.

The problem was no longer whether No-Name needed to leave Farhaven.

He did.

The problem was how.

Their preferred destination was the **Ebonmurk**, the great swamp near Duskwatch. It was wild, isolated, and considerably less likely to attract the attention of dwarven excavators convinced that No-Name represented some kind of infestation.

Getting him there was another matter.

The party considered their options.

They could charter a wagon.

Buy one.

Borrow one through someone they knew.

And, if all else failed, Surang was reasonably confident that he could simply build one.

There was only one problem with that final option.

Time.

Because Farhaven was about to give them considerably less of it than they expected.

## Professor Garr and the History of Hamyrhal

Professor Garr agreed to meet the party later that evening at the Wayfarer’s Refuge.

Over dinner, the professor began filling in pieces of dwarven history that had survived only as fragments during the party’s exploration beneath Farhaven.

He spoke of the ancient clans.

Of Hamyrhal.

Of the beginnings of Fel’Garrak.

And of the line that would eventually produce the Mountain-Kings.

For Garr, Hamyrhal was not merely an archaeological discovery.

It was history that had been buried beneath the city for centuries.

Which made what was currently happening there all the more troubling.

The excavation, Garr explained, was being directed by **Stone Marshal Dagna Koll**.

He did not understand the urgency behind her operation.

Whatever historical importance Hamyrhal possessed, Dagna seemed interested in something much more immediate.

She was impatient.

Aggressively so.

Under her authority, the ancient golems the party had once struggled to survive had simply been destroyed.

No-Name’s cavern had been razed.

And the excavation continued pressing deeper toward the sealed Forge.

Garr had been assigned to determine how the Forge could be opened.

He increasingly suspected that reaching it was the real purpose of Dagna’s work.

Surang listened.

And began putting pieces together.

## The Hammer

The symbol had been appearing everywhere.

Fel’Hamyr.

The Forge.

The old iconography.

Again and again, the hammer.

Surang asked Garr a very specific question.

Did the sealed Forge door have some kind of keyhole?

A recess?

A relief?

Something shaped like a hammer?

Garr looked at him.

Yes.

It did.

The sealed Forge door bore a **hammer-shaped relief**.

Somewhere within the mysteries surrounding Hamyrhal the implication settled over the conversation.

The door had not been opened.

But for the first time, the sealed Forge no longer felt quite so impossible.

## Eltharion’s Revised Scrolls

After dinner, the party went upstairs to meet with Eltharion.

He had been working.

Again.

This should perhaps have concerned them.

Eltharion produced another collection of **Disguise Self** scrolls, revised from his previous attempts.

The first disguises had been terrible.

These were—

somehow—

worse.

![DisguiseSelf3.png\|800](/img/user/z_Assets/DisguiseSelf3.png)

For a time, the party was subjected to another collection of deeply cursed attempts at looking inconspicuous.

Eventually, after further adjustment, Eltharion produced something the group was willing to describe as functional.

Not elegant.

Not reassuring.

Functional.

He also provided the additional scrolls he had prepared for their coming journey and offered to make more if they identified anything specific they might need.

When the subject turned to No-Name, Eltharion offered practical help as well.

If the party needed a wagon, he would try to find one.

That gave them another possible way to move No-Name out of the city.

More importantly, it meant they might not have to ask Surang to become a wagonwright on extremely short notice.

## The Hamyrhal Lockbox

There was still one more mystery from beneath Farhaven waiting to be opened.

The sealed box recovered from Hamyrhal.

Eltharion had provided a **Knock** scroll.

Needle provided noise.

A great deal of noise.

While Needle created the distraction, the others tested the spell.

The lockbox opened.

Inside were several sachets.

Tea.

Magical tea, apparently.

After everything required to open the box, this was perhaps not what anyone expected.

What the tea actually did remained another question entirely.

For now, the party kept it.

They had accumulated enough mysterious magical objects to know that casually consuming things found in ancient dwarven lockboxes was probably best approached with at least a small amount of caution.

Probably.

## Night at the Wayfarer’s Refuge

Eventually, the Wayfarer’s Refuge settled into something resembling sleep.

The party did not.

Not entirely.

### Surang

Surang returned to his shield.

The Bulette reinforcement had made it stronger, but the shield was becoming something more than improved equipment.

He continued carving into its surface the image that had stayed with him since Hamyrhal:

the dragon.

The mountain.

The mural buried beneath Farhaven.

Bit by bit, Surang was carrying the things he encountered forward with him.

Literally.

### Linley

Nearby, Linley practiced something considerably newer.

Magic.

His first steps into arcane spellcraft had only recently begun, and the work still required concentration.

For once, nobody exploded.

Progress.

### Needle

Needle slept.

And dreamed.

At first, the dream was beautiful.

Fey children.

Families.

Life together.

Warmth.

Joy.

Then it changed.

The same figures lay dead.

Screams filled the dream.

Devastation replaced the happiness that had come before it.

Needle woke in distress.

Clio responded the way Clio so often did when words were insufficient.

She played.

The sound of her lyre gradually calmed him.

Eventually, Needle settled.

Eventually, he slept again.

The dream offered no explanation.

No certainty.

Only another fragment pressing against the places in Needle’s memory that remained missing.

Whatever it meant, if it meant anything at all, would have to wait.

# The Following Day

## Sixfold Overload

The next morning, Linley and Needle made their way to the Radlocks.

Linley’s firearm was ready.

The weapon had been modified so that all six loaded rounds could be discharged at once, unleashing the entire chamber in a single explosive burst of lightning.

**Sixfold Overload.**

Linley was delighted.

There are moments when a craftsman knows immediately that they have understood their customer.

This was one of them.

## Trixie Has an Idea

Unfortunately, collecting the firearm also meant giving Trixie an opportunity to explain one of her own projects.

Farhaven was short on Wardens.

Its defenses were strained.

This was, genuinely, a serious problem.

Trixie had developed a solution.

![FARHAVEN HEROES.png\|800](/img/user/z_Assets/FARHAVEN%20HEROES.png)

A heroic squad.

Brightly coordinated.

Visually unified.

Distinct identities.

Apparently some degree of dramatic posing.

The presentation proceeded about as well as anyone might reasonably expect.

The idea was absurd.

Completely absurd.

But beneath all of the color, enthusiasm, and unmistakable Trixie-ness of the proposal was something sincere.

Farhaven needed people willing to protect it.

Trixie wanted to help.

This was simply the language in which Trixie understood helping.

## Clio Searches for an Instrument

Elsewhere, Clio had a problem of her own.

The Lantern Festival was approaching.

She intended to perform.

She needed an instrument.

Preferably something strange.

Farhaven’s merchants did not seem to understand the assignment.

Most were selling useful things.

Practical things.

Things ordinary people might reasonably want to purchase.

Clio continued searching.

Eventually, she adapted.

If the markets would not provide a suitably unusual instrument—

she would improvise.

Clio purchased:

a washboard.

And a wooden spoon.

Then she found an alley.

And began practicing.

Somewhere in Farhaven, a future audience remained blissfully unaware.

## The Boundary Anvil

Surang returned to the Boundary Anvil to collect the work made from the Bulette spoils.

His trophies were ready.

A fang had been fashioned into a necklace.

More importantly, a section of Bulette plate had been incorporated into his shield, reinforcing it with the armor of the creature the party had killed in the mountains.

Master Korrim Vale was there.

And Korrim remembered Linley.

Very clearly.

> “The ugly little dwarf who tried to cheat me.”

Surang did not attempt to defend Linley with excuses.

He simply answered honestly.

Something about that earned Korrim’s respect.

The smith offered Surang a discount.

Part of it was framed as something between brothers.

Part of it was thanks for what Surang had done for the miners trapped during the Bulette attack—people who would never be able to repay him themselves.

Then the conversation returned to Linley.

Korrim learned a little more about the dwarf’s circumstances.

His response was characteristically warm.

Which is to say it was not warm at all.

> “If your friend is still naked the next time you see him, send him back to me.”

A moment later, the gruffness softened just enough for the truth beneath it to show.

> “I know what it’s like to have nothing.”

Korrim remained Korrim.

Abrupt.

Abrasive.

Entirely capable of remembering Linley first and foremost as the ugly little dwarf who tried to cheat him.

But his compassion, when it appeared, did not come in kind words.

It came in work.

In discounts.

In armor.

In making sure someone who had nothing did not stay that way.

## The Shield

Before Surang left, Korrim tested his work.

The smith struck the reinforced shield.

Surang caught the blow.

Deflected it.

Held firm.

The moment tied together more than a new piece of equipment.

The technique Surang had learned from Amira.

The shield bash he had adapted from her sweeping maneuver.

The Bulette plate taken from the creature they defeated in the mountains.

Korrim’s craftsmanship.

And now Surang’s ability to use all of it as one.

The lesson was complete.

Surang had grown stronger.

## Two Days

Eventually, the party reunited and returned to the Council Hall.

Harrow Blackwall had news.

Preparations for the Lantern Festival were progressing.

The celebration would take place in **two days**.

Two days.

That changed the problem.

No-Name was still hidden beneath Farhaven.

The party still needed transportation.

They still needed a route.

They still needed to get him out without Fel’Garrak realizing what they were doing.

And if they intended to use the Festival itself as cover, then there would be no leisurely preparation.

No time for Surang to spend several days building a wagon.

No room for indefinite planning.

They had two days to make the escape work.

## Return to the Broken Standard

The party’s search for supplies and transportation eventually brought them back to the Broken Standard.

The guild could provide the travel supplies they would need.

A wagon remained another matter.

While there, they also checked on Elyza.

The Bulette was dead.

The quarry had been survived.

Everyone had come home.

That did not mean everyone was fine.

Elyza was still visibly shaken by what had happened in the mountains.

The guildhall had moved on to preparations, trophies, repairs, and whatever crisis would come next.

Elyza had not.

The party left the Broken Standard with supplies available, several possible leads for transportation, and a deadline hanging over everything.

Two days until the Lantern Festival.

Two days to get No-Name out of Farhaven.

Korrim tested the reinforced shield by striking it.

With the shield completed and his Shield Master training narratively realized, Surang ascended to **level four**.

His advancement is now canonically tied to:

- Amira’s sweeping maneuver;    
- Surang adapting it into a shield bash;    
- the creation of his Bulette-reinforced shield;    
- his successful defense against Korrim’s test.
