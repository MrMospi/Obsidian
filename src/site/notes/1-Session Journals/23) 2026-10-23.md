# Session 23 — Into the Glasslands

## A Night at the Lantern Spire

At the Lantern Spire Inn, Linley and Needle were discovering that the establishment's accommodations came at a rather considerable price. Rather than endure the expense without complaint, the pair devised a plan to supplement their funds at the expense of their fellow patrons.

Needle would provide a distraction. Linley would handle the clientele.

The pixie launched into an enthusiastic performance on the spoons, apparently convinced that his musical talents deserved an audience. While the room was subjected to his percussion, Linley adopted the persona of an exotic traveler from Veyrasa and began ingratiating himself with the other guests.

The scheme proved surprisingly productive.

During his conversations with Ebon and Martha Pole, two lesser nobles who seemed to have an uncomfortable relationship to Lord Howe. For a dwarf with a personal history involving the man, the information was worth remembering.

Needle, meanwhile, found an opportunity to exercise a different set of talents. Amid the commotion, he managed to pickpocket a billfold containing a list of debtors and outstanding IOUs.

Their evening of recreational larceny was going rather well.

![EmptyWallet.png](/img/user/z_Assets/EmptyWallet.png)

Then Clio returned.

The young bard took one look at her companions' activities and immediately recognized that trouble was brewing. She ushered the pair toward a secluded corner table, where Linley's questionable behavior and history with Howe became the subjects of a rather animated discussion.

Clio's objections did little to change the fact that her companions had already acquired both intelligence and someone else's financial records.

When Surang eventually returned, Clio asked Bartlett, the innkeeper, to show them to their room.

There was another matter troubling the party, however, and it had nothing to do with the inn's prices.

Needle had been struggling to sleep.

His nights had become plagued by nightmares, leaving him exhausted even when he managed to rest. Clio attempted to help by playing her lyre, soothing the little pixie with music until he finally drifted off.

For a time, the treatment appeared successful.

But sleep brought no lasting relief. Needle awoke from another nightmare, his rest disturbed by whatever continued to haunt his dreams.

Later that evening, after Linley had retired, Surang and Clio remained awake to discuss something that had been occupying the dragonborn's thoughts.

## A Tam Lin of Sanctuary

Surang had been considering the legacy of **Eothar**, the legendary Knight of Justice whose death had become one of Thurandil's most enduring stories.

He wondered whether Eothar's armor and title might be claimed, and whether a different kind of Tam Lin could be established in opposition to the Queen.

Not another servant of the throne, but a benevolent protector.

A **Tam Lin Knight of Sanctuary**.

![TamLinSanctuary.png](/img/user/z_Assets/TamLinSanctuary.png)

Surang imagined what might happen if word spread of such a knight, one whose authority came not from the Queen's appointment but from the protection he offered to those in need.

It was an ambitious idea, particularly for someone who had only recently begun to understand the reach of Thurandil and its legendary champions.

Clio found the proposal intriguing, but she was far less certain about its wisdom.

The Tam Lin were no ordinary knights. Their strength and the authority they wielded were considerable, and even Clio, who had grown up within Thurandil, understood the danger of provoking them.

She warned Surang against underestimating the Queen's champions or assuming that the party could withstand a confrontation with them.

There was also the matter of what the others knew. Linley and Needle had little familiarity with elven history or the political consequences of challenging the Tam Lin.

Such an undertaking would require more than courage.

Before committing themselves to anything, Clio suggested they seek Eltharion's counsel and learn as much as possible about the legacy Surang hoped to claim.

Their conversation eventually turned toward more personal matters, and Surang shared the meaning of his name.

_Sanctuary._

It was a fitting name for the oath he had sworn and the path he now contemplated.

The two eventually retired, Clio settling into her meditation as the inn grew quiet around them.

## A Little Shopping Before the Desert

Clio was the first to rise the following morning, greeted by the rather unfortunate sight of a thoroughly bedraggled Needle.

Whatever sleep he had managed to obtain had done little to restore him.

After gathering their belongings, the party made its way toward the town square, where the morning's business was already beginning.

Clio spotted **Maurice Honoratus** opening his bakery and led the companions inside. Maurice welcomed them warmly, and the group also met **Perrin Mott**, with whom they discussed the changing character of Il'Madris.

The city's growing wealth and transformation had become increasingly apparent to those who lived and worked there, and the conversation offered another glimpse of the changes taking place beyond the party's immediate concerns.

Breakfast was available at a reasonable thirteen gold pieces.

When Clio announced that she intended to pay for everyone, the total mysteriously fell to ten.

A curious development, but one the party was unlikely to protest.

Surang visited Perrin's flower cart, where he purchased a lily of the valley. Linley, meanwhile, sought out an apothecary, hoping to obtain a sleeping potion for Needle.

The pixie's nightmares had become persistent enough that the party was now exploring remedies beyond Clio's music. Linley hoped the potion might finally allow him to enjoy an uninterrupted night's rest.

With their errands concluded, the companions gathered and made their way toward the northern entrance of Il'Madris.

They had a long journey ahead.

And, as it turned out, an unexpected traveling companion.

## An Uninvited Companion

As the party approached the city's northern exit, they became aware of someone following them.

The stranger was an unusual fellow, and his attempts at conversation immediately presented a problem.

Nobody could understand what he was saying.

Clio tried casting _Comprehend Languages_, but even the spell failed to make sense of his speech.

The stranger appeared entirely untroubled by this inconvenience.

Reaching into his belongings, he produced a collection of pills, swallowed them, and promptly began speaking perfectly understandable Common.

He introduced himself as a traveling companion, though he proved remarkably evasive about precisely why he had attached himself to their group.

When questioned about his destination, he refused to offer a straightforward answer. He spoke in riddles, suggesting familiarity with their journey without explaining how he had acquired it.

He called himself the **Medicine Seller**.

![The Medicine Seller.png](/img/user/z_Assets/The%20Medicine%20Seller.png)

The party had not invited him along, and his apparent determination to accompany them was met with understandable suspicion.

They attempted to leave him behind, weaving through the streets in the hope of putting some distance between themselves and their peculiar follower.

It accomplished very little.

The Medicine Seller simply continued after them, seemingly unconcerned by their efforts to escape his company.

At one point, he stopped beside a merchant's stall, selected some fruit, and helped himself.

He did not appear to pay.

More strangely, nobody seemed to notice.

![MedSell2.png](/img/user/z_Assets/MedSell2.png)

Clio found the man's behavior sufficiently suspicious to investigate with _Detect Thoughts_. Whatever she expected to discover, the spell revealed nothing obviously amiss.

The stranger continued behaving as though his presence required no explanation, offering little more than cryptic remarks when questioned.

Unable to discourage him, the party finally departed Il'Madris with the Medicine Seller following close behind.

Clio played her bagpipes as they traveled, bringing a rather distinctive musical accompaniment to their departure.

Ahead lay the desert.

And beyond its shifting dunes, the Glasslands.

## Where Sand Becomes Glass

The desert stretched toward the horizon, broken only by distant dunes and the immense storm swirling at its center.

The disturbance was impossible to ignore. It dominated the landscape, a vast churning mass of wind and dust that seemed to have established itself permanently over the heart of the region.

As the party advanced, the terrain grew increasingly hostile.

The dunes rose higher, the winds strengthened, and the loose sands beneath their feet gradually gave way to the glass that had given the region its name.

Clio suggested that the companions tie themselves together, using Surang as their anchor against the worsening conditions.

The dragonborn's formidable strength made him the obvious choice, and the others secured themselves to him before proceeding.

At first, the wind carried little more than sand.

Then came the glass.

Fragments of it whipped through the air, driven by gusts powerful enough to stagger the travelers. The further they progressed, the more dangerous each step became.

The party employed climbing equipment and lightning stakes to help secure their passage, attempting to maintain their footing as the storm battered them from every direction.

Clio relied upon her climbing spikes to negotiate the increasingly treacherous terrain, but even with their precautions, the desert exacted its toll.

Then lightning struck.

A bolt tore through the storm and caught Clio, delivering sixteen points of lightning damage.

The impact left her badly injured, and she sought shelter behind Surang as the companions struggled against the relentless wind.

![ThroughTheGlasslands.png](/img/user/z_Assets/ThroughTheGlasslands.png)

She managed to pull Linley away from danger, helping him regain his footing before the storm could scatter them.

Linley, displaying a surprising talent for navigating the chaos, identified an alternate route and directed the party through the shifting dunes.

Clio followed, though by this point she had lost almost all sense of their position and direction.

Surang remained their anchor, bracing himself against the gale while the others struggled to advance.

Even his strength, however, had limits.

A particularly violent gust overwhelmed the dragonborn, sending him buckling beneath the force of the storm and dragging his tethered companions down with him.

For a moment, the party was forced to reconsider its approach.

Should they continue?

Clio and Linley agreed that they had little choice but to press forward.

With renewed determination, they fought their way through the winds. Clio danced across the treacherous ground, helping Linley regain his position and assisting Surang as he struggled to keep the group together.

The desert seemed determined to drive them back, but they continued until a promising shape emerged through the blowing sand.

A great dune rose above the surrounding landscape, its towering slope offering some protection from the worst of the storm.

Beneath it, they found enough shelter to establish a temporary camp.

For the first time since entering the Glasslands, the companions had an opportunity to rest.

Their mysterious traveling companion, however, had something to say about the place they had entered.

## The Medicine Seller's Warning

The Medicine Seller regarded the storm with an unsettling familiarity.

Where the others saw a violent natural phenomenon, he seemed to recognize something far more complicated.

He began to speak.

>*"The storm has swallowed these lands."*
>*"Yet no matter how fiercely it rages, it goes nowhere."*
>*"It circles. Returns. Begins again."*
>*"This storm does not wander. It turns upon itself."*
>*"And it does not turn alone."*
>*"The dead are carried within it."*
>*"Round and round they are borne—through sand, through storm, through death."*
>*"Churned onward, denied their rest."*
>*"This… is the Form."*

His words suggested that the storm was no ordinary disturbance.

Something within it was trapped in a cycle, caught between death and whatever forces continued to drive the storm onward.

Surang called upon his divine senses, attempting to determine whether something supernatural was present.

The sensation that answered him was deeply unsettling.

Something about the surrounding land felt profoundly unnatural, though the source of that wrongness remained unclear.

The Medicine Seller questioned the companions about their intended destination and what they understood of the Glasslands.

Their answers appeared to interest him.

At last, he reached among his belongings and produced an ornate sword, its form wrapped in three red ribbons.

The weapon was unlike anything the party had seen him carry before.

He called it a **Sword of Exorcism**.

>*"The Sword of Exorcism cannot be drawn freely. Three conditions must first be known: Form, Truth, and Regret."*
>*"Form, we have found."*
>*"The intertwined fates of the living give the dead their Form."*
>*"Truth is what remains when illusion, memory, and falsehood are stripped away."*
>*"And Regret…"*
>*"…is rarely difficult to find in the hearts of men."*

The sword remained sheathed.

Whatever purpose it served, and whatever relationship its three conditions bore to the storm, the Medicine Seller offered no further explanation.

Instead, he turned his attention to the exhausted travelers.

>*"My friends, take rest where you can. The desert demands much of us still."*

The party settled beneath the sheltering dune, surrounded by the distant roar of the storm.

Somewhere beyond them lay the heart of the Glasslands, where the dead were said to wander without rest.

And whatever awaited them there, the Medicine Seller seemed to know more than he was prepared to reveal.
