# Session Recap — Always Been Here

## Breakfast at the Greyfoam Roost

Morning came in Duskwatch, and the companions gathered at the Greyfoam Roost to prepare for their departure.

Tallion Briarwisp, in an uncharacteristic display of generosity, had provided rations for their journey. He even described them as _free_, a word whose meaning the party had learned to regard with some suspicion whenever it came from the half-fey tavernkeeper.

Over breakfast, the four travelers finally found an opportunity to learn more about one another.

Clio spoke of **Thurandil**, the great elven kingdom from which she hailed. Though she had already spent considerable time traveling beyond its borders, much of the wider world remained unfamiliar to her, and she was eager to discover what lay beyond the places she knew.

Stillness had come from a village far to the north, he remembered his life in the northern mountains clearly. He also recalled strange dreams in which something called out to him, accompanied by visions of a dark crystal.

But what happened after he left his village was far less certain. His passage through the Great Forest of Somn remained a blur, leaving him unable to explain what had transpired before he eventually emerged near Duskwatch.

Needle's childhood had been considerably less pleasant.

The little pixie had been taken by giants when he was very young, and his memories of his parents were faint. His captors had kept him as a pet rather than treating him as a person, and their treatment of him had been anything but kind.

For a creature who had demonstrated such extraordinary ferocity since his escape, Needle's account offered a glimpse into a life that had given him little reason to trust those larger than himself.

Linley, meanwhile, hailed from **Fel'Garrak**, the great dwarven capital that had so thoroughly captured Clio's interest. He was searching for his cousin Varrick, a merchant whom he hoped to find in the village of Dunwich.

With a destination established and their supplies gathered, the party departed Duskwatch alongside Torvald and his faithful donkey, Invincible.

## The Road to Dunwich

Torvald had accompanied the party through their troubles in the Ebonmurk, and his familiarity with the surrounding countryside made him a welcome traveling companion.

As their journey continued, however, the knight shared some troubling news.

Dunwich had been cut off from the surrounding settlements. Ordinary travel and communication had become unreliable, and reports from the village had grown scarce. Torvald suspected bandits might be responsible, though he had little evidence to explain the situation.

The companions approached with caution, expecting they might encounter trouble along the road or find the settlement occupied by brigands.

Instead, they found Dunwich standing much as it always had.

The village appeared intact. Its buildings remained undisturbed, and the streets were occupied by townspeople going about their daily routines.

Yet the inhabitants behaved strangely.

They moved with an unnatural detachment, continuing their activities with little apparent awareness of their surroundings. Attempts to engage them revealed an unsettling absence of recognition, as though the villagers were caught in some waking dream.

Torvald was particularly disturbed by their behavior. The townspeople seemed to be in a trance, and whatever had caused it had apparently affected much of the settlement.

Needle attempted to investigate in his own fashion.

Spotting a little girl among the inhabitants, the pixie flew toward her, apparently intending to get her attention.

The child ran straight through him.

There was no collision. No startled reaction. She simply passed through the space he occupied.

Whatever was afflicting Dunwich was clearly beyond the work of ordinary bandits.

The companions continued their investigation, searching for someone capable of explaining what had happened to the villagers. Their efforts eventually brought them to the local tavern.

There, they encountered a man named **Varnoth**.

## Always Been Here

Varnoth seemed entirely at home in Dunwich.

Unlike the entranced villagers outside, the stranger appeared perfectly lucid, carrying himself with the easy confidence of a man who had neither a concern in the world nor any particular reason to acquire one.

His presence, however, raised an immediate question.

Who was he?

As the party sought to understand his relationship with the village, they encountered a peculiar certainty surrounding him. Varnoth was not regarded as a recent arrival, nor did his presence seem to invite any suspicion among the townspeople.

He had simply always been there.

The man himself offered little assistance in unraveling the mystery. Rather than explaining the condition of Dunwich, he seemed far more interested in entertaining his visitors.

He proposed a drinking game.

It was an unusual invitation, particularly under the circumstances, but Varnoth proved an engaging host. His jovial manner stood in stark contrast to the strange, lifeless behavior of the villagers, and he appeared entirely unconcerned by the questions his guests had brought with them.

The companions agreed to play.

As the game progressed, however, the nature of their host became increasingly difficult to ignore.

**Varnoth was a devil.**

![Varnoth2.png](/img/user/z_Assets/Varnoth2.png)

The realization cast his hospitality in an altogether different light.

What had initially appeared to be an eccentric drinking game was an opportunity for Varnoth to conduct business, and the currency he sought was far more intimate than gold.

He wanted memories.

Not stories that might be shared over a drink, but the memories themselves: pieces of a person's life, surrendered in exchange for whatever payment the devil offered.

There was something particularly cruel about such a bargain. A person might part with a treasured possession and continue to remember what it meant to them. Varnoth's proposition threatened to take the experience itself, leaving its former owner without the very recollection that made it precious.

Stillness was deeply suspicious of the arrangement.

The silver dragonborn wanted no part of Varnoth's game and withdrew from the encounter, unwilling to entrust any portion of his past to a devil.

The others remained, drawn further into the strange negotiations.

Varnoth continued offering his bargains, attempting to convince his guests that the things he could provide were worth the memories they would surrender.

And eventually, someone accepted.

**Needle gave Varnoth one of his memories.**

What the devil took from the little pixie was his to keep, and Needle already suffered from a fractured recollection of his past. Varnoth took the memories of Needle's giant family, leaving a hole in his heart as the memories were burned away.

The bargain was struck, but the party would soon discover that the price of their encounter extended far beyond anything exchanged within the tavern.

When the rest of the party refused the Devil's bargains he gave an exaggerated sigh.

>***"How disappointing..."***

## Ten

When the companions emerged from the tavern, Dunwich had changed.

The villagers who had wandered through the streets in their strange, dreamlike trance were disappearing.

Not fleeing their homes or hiding from some approaching threat.

Simply vanishing.

As though they had been plucked from existence.

By the time the terrible phenomenon had run its course, approximately three quarters of the town's population had disappeared without a trace.

Only ten inhabitants remained.

The party had entered Dunwich expecting to investigate bandits. Instead, they had encountered a devil who traded in memories and witnessed a catastrophe.

The surviving villagers were left to contend with the loss of their families, neighbors, and nearly everyone they had known.

Among those who remained was a man whose daughter, **Lyra**, appeared to have some connection to the strange trance that had overtaken the town.

Through their conversation with him, the companions learned that Lyra had been responsible for the condition of the villagers. Born sickly, the village traded pieces of themselves as payment to save the young girl's life. 

Though now it seems that the villagers traded far more than they could have possibly realized.

The party spent time assisting the survivors, tending to those who required healing and speaking with the remaining townspeople in an effort to understand what had occurred.

There was little they could do for those who had vanished.

Whatever Varnoth had accomplished in Dunwich, its consequences would remain long after the devil's drinking game had ended.

Torvald, confronted with the suffering of the survivors, decided that his place was among them.

The Knight of the Flame of Fellowship had accompanied the party into the Ebonmurk and helped them survive their first dangerous expedition. Now he found himself facing a different responsibility.

Dunwich needed help.

He would remain behind to provide it and eventually lead the survivors to a place where they could be safe.

Having failed to find Linley's cousin, the companions bid farewell to the knight and his faithful Invincible, leaving the village in his care as they resumed their journey toward Farhaven.

Their road, however, had one more unpleasant surprise in store.

## Lady Cliovara

The party's journey brought them into the path of another traveler, a Half-Elf named **Lothaniel Silverleaf**. He was accompanied by a small contingent of armed men, 
two of which were the guardsman that had been driving the prison wagon that contained Linley and Needle. 

![LothanielBiggsWedge.png](/img/user/z_Assets/LothanielBiggsWedge.png)

Unlike most of the people they had encountered since leaving Duskwatch, Lothaniel immediately recognized the significance of Clio's heritage.

Her name carried weight among the High Elves, and the stranger appeared impressed to find a daughter of House Aurelith traveling through the region.

His courtesy, unfortunately, did not extend to her companions.

Lothaniel demanded payment for passage, treating the dwarf, dragonborn, and pixie with considerably less respect than he had afforded Clio.

The young bard took offense at his attitude.

Whatever the elf thought of her companions, Clio had no intention of allowing him to mistreat them merely because they lacked the noble lineage he apparently held in such high regard.

Lothaniel responded to her objections with an extraordinary conclusion.

**Lady Cliovara was in distress.**

Clearly, the noblewoman had been abducted by the assortment of unsavory individuals accompanying her, and her present behavior could only be the result of her unfortunate circumstances.

That Clio herself was defending these supposed captors did little to discourage him.

Lothaniel had apparently decided that the situation required heroic intervention.

He would rescue Lady Cliovara.

From her friends.

The half-elf declared his intentions, and the confrontation quickly descended into violence.

![MeetingLothaniel.png](/img/user/z_Assets/MeetingLothaniel.png)

## An Unwelcome Rescue

Lothaniel proved considerably more dangerous than his ridiculous misunderstanding might have suggested.

Armed with a rapier bearing an unusual enchantment, he engaged the party with enough skill to make the encounter genuinely threatening.

Clio attempted to defend her companions, but the fighting nearly brought her down at the hands of the very half-elf who had declared himself her rescuer.

Stillness intervened.

Calling upon the divine power at his command, the silver dragonborn struck Lothaniel with a devastating smite.

The blow was enough to convince the rogue that his rescue mission had become considerably more dangerous than anticipated.

Lothaniel fled, abandoning the confrontation and leaving his would-be charge in precisely the same company in which he had found her.

The party recovered several possessions in the aftermath: a signet ring, a bag of counterfeit gold, and the enchanted rapier.

They also acquired a bottle of exceptionally fine elven moonshine.

Considering that Lothaniel had begun the encounter demanding payment from them, the outcome was a rather spectacular reversal of fortune.

Clio, meanwhile, remained safely in the custody of her alleged kidnappers.

Whether Lothaniel would ever recover from the humiliation of his unsuccessful rescue remained to be seen.

For the companions, the encounter marked another milestone.

**The party reached Level 3.**

With Lothaniel driven from the road and Dunwich behind them, they resumed their journey toward Farhaven, an ancient dwarven stronghold that had grown into one of Sulimar's great cities.

For Linley, it promised new opportunities. For Clio, a wealth of knowledge and unfamiliar sights. And for Stillness, still searching for direction and purpose, Farhaven would soon offer something he had long struggled to find.

A path beneath his feet.
