# Session Recap

## The Broken Standard and the Bulette Spoils

With the Bulette dead and the expedition behind them, Surang, Linley, and Needle set about finding uses for what they had managed to carve from its remains.

The materials were divided among Farhaven’s craftsmen for what they hoped would become meaningful upgrades, with the three agreeing to regroup afterward at the Broken Standard.

When they arrived, the guildhall was already undergoing an upgrade of its own.

Several of the Standard’s initiates, with Garrick’s assistance, were attempting to mount the enormous Bulette skull inside the building.

Apparently killing the creature had not been enough.

Now they intended to decorate with it.

While the work continued, Surang found Amira.

He had not forgotten the sweeping maneuver she had demonstrated during their time in the mountains, and Amira was willing to show him again.

Surang watched carefully.

Rather than reproduce the technique exactly, he adapted its principle to his own fighting style—turning the movement into a powerful shield bash designed to knock an opponent from their feet.

The lesson worked.

Surang had found the technique he had been looking for.

Linley, meanwhile, reached an entirely different conclusion about what had just happened.

As far as he was concerned, Amira had been flirting.

Surang had somehow failed to notice.

This was unacceptable.

Linley therefore took it upon himself to explain the situation and, apparently without receiving any formal appointment, assumed the role of Surang’s romantic adviser.

Surang now had a new combat technique.

And, considerably less helpfully, Linley.

Before they left, Amira passed along another piece of good news.

With the Bulette crisis finally resolved, the danger that had forced Farhaven to delay the Lantern Festival was gone.

The Festival would soon proceed.

For the first time in several days, Farhaven had reason to prepare for something other than disaster.

## Eltharion’s Findings

Elsewhere in the city, Clio had considerably less cheerful work ahead of her.

She joined Eltharion and Ilyra in the Archive, where Eltharion had continued digging through Thurandil’s restricted history.

What he had found was not one isolated atrocity.

It was a pattern.

Again and again, Thurandil’s expansion had been accompanied by violence.

Again and again, the records surrounding that violence had been obscured, incomplete, or carefully softened.

And again and again, the same figures appeared nearby.

The Tam Lin.

Until now, the name had existed mostly at the edges of the party’s understanding.

That was beginning to change.

Eltharion believed the legendary knights had repeatedly been deployed during some of Thurandil’s most troubling campaigns, and he suspected that the next answers might be found in the Glasslands.

Eventually, the long archival session came to an end.

Eltharion had removed a tremendous amount of material during the investigation.

Clio decided to help put it away.

With magic.

Ilyra watched.

And was horrified.

The books and records were returned.

Technically.

Later that evening, Clio gathered the others and explained what Eltharion had discovered.

Thurandil’s history was not as clean as it had been presented.

The Tam Lin were not merely legendary knights from old stories.

They were extraordinarily dangerous agents of Thurandil who seemed to appear repeatedly alongside the kingdom’s expansion and bloodshed.

And somewhere in the Glasslands, Eltharion believed, there might be more answers.

The Tam Lin were no longer simply names buried in old records.

Now the entire party knew who they were.

## Eltharion’s Scrolls

Eltharion was not finished contributing to the party’s growing collection of questionable solutions.

Later, he brought them into his room and produced several scrolls he had prepared during his escape from Thurandil.

Among them were scrolls intended to cast Disguise Self.

Useful.

Potentially extremely useful.

The party tested them.

The disguises were terrible.

Not ineffective, exactly.

Just catastrophically unhelpful.

The resulting dwarven appearances did very little to inspire confidence in Eltharion’s work.

Eltharion, to his credit, acknowledged the problem.

He would try again.

For the moment, however, the disastrous dwarf disguises were real.

They had happened.

And unfortunately for everyone involved, they could not be unseen.

## Clio’s Dark Scroll

That night, another object demanded Clio’s attention.

The dark scroll recovered from the Archive.

Something about it had been pulling at her.

Eventually, Clio opened it.

The scroll reacted immediately.

Its contents overlaid themselves upon her existing map before the two objects physically—or magically—merged together.

Then the map changed.

A location had been marked.

Circled.

The same place Torvald had previously identified simply as:

**Bad.**

The party already had one destination drawing them outward from Farhaven.

The Glasslands.

Now Clio’s map had given them another.

And unlike Eltharion’s careful historical conclusions, this one felt considerably more personal.

## The Scarf Witch

The following morning, the party made their way toward the Council Hall to report the success of the Bulette expedition to Harrow.

Along the way, they encountered someone familiar.

The Scarf Witch.

She greeted most of the party warmly enough.

Then she looked at Clio.

> “I don be havin’ nuffin for ye, knife-ear.”

Clio, apparently, was not part of today’s business.

The conversation continued.

At some point, the Scarf Witch offered one of her strange cultural sayings and extended an open hand toward the party.

Linley did not hesitate.

He placed seventy gold into it.

> “I’m not messing with any of that.”

Whatever lesson, bargain, custom, omen, or danger might have been involved, Linley had decided that seventy gold was an acceptable price for not finding out.

The encounter left the party with the same problem they had possessed before.

They still did not know who the Scarf Witch truly was.

They still did not understand her history.

But with every meeting, it was becoming more difficult to dismiss the power behind her strange little stand.

## Harrow and Professor Garr

Eventually, the party reached the Council Hall.

There, they reported the outcome of the Bulette hunt to Harrow Blackwall.

The creature was dead.

The crisis was over.

Harrow thanked them and confirmed what Amira had already suggested: the severity of the attacks had forced Farhaven to postpone the Lantern Festival.

Now that the threat had been removed, preparations could resume.

But Harrow had someone else he wanted them to meet.

Professor Garr of Fel’Garrak.

Garr had come to Farhaven because of something considerably older than the Bulette.

Hamyrhal.

The party had rediscovered an ancient dwarven city beneath Farhaven.

Professor Garr very much wanted to hear from the people who had found it.

At first, the conversation was academic.

Then someone asked about No-Name.

Garr changed.

## What Happened to No-Name

The professor did not answer immediately.

Eventually, Garr led the party away from the Council Hall and down through another route beneath the city, toward the sewer approaches connected to Hamyrhal.

There, hidden away in the place the party had once suggested would be safe, they found No-Name.

Alive.

Wounded.

And furious.

The mushroom cavern where they had first encountered him was gone.

The dwarves exploring Hamyrhal had found the fungal growth spreading through their ancestral city and interpreted it as an infection.

Something invasive.

Something corrupting a place that belonged to their people.

So they destroyed it.

They burned the cavern.

They burned the fungus.

They burned No-Name’s home.

Through the dying spore network, Professor Garr had heard fragments of what was happening.

Pain.

Rage.

Fear.

Enough to understand that whatever the dwarves believed they were cleansing, there was a living intelligence caught inside it.

Garr eventually found the surviving Myconid and hid him here.

Now the party stood before him again.

But only one of them could truly hear him.

During their first encounter with No-Name, Needle had eaten one of his mushrooms.

Because of that connection, Needle alone could directly receive what the Myconid was trying to communicate.

And No-Name had a great deal to say.

His home was gone.

His fungal community was gone.

The place he had tended had been destroyed by strangers who never understood what lived there.

Needle felt his rage.

His grief.

His displacement.

And, beneath all of it, betrayal.

The party had once told No-Name that this place could be safe.

It was not.

They could not undo what had happened.

But they could decide what happened next.

For the moment, they reinforced the hiding place as best they could.

Then they began planning.

No-Name needed to leave Farhaven.

And somehow, without the dwarves discovering him, the party intended to get him out.



# Session Overview

### The Broken Standard and the Bulette Spoils

Surang, Linley, and Needle delivered their harvested Bulette materials to be made into meaningful upgrades, agreeing to reunite at the Broken Standard.

At the guildhall, the Standard’s initiates—assisted by Garrick—were attempting to mount the enormous Bulette skull inside the building.

Amira demonstrated her sweeping maneuver for Surang again. Surang adapted the principle into a shield bash capable of knocking an opponent prone, satisfying the narrative requirement for **Shield Master**.

Linley interpreted Amira’s instruction as flirting and criticized Surang for failing to notice it and has nevertheless appointed himself Surang’s romantic adviser.

Before the group departed, Amira informed them that the Lantern Festival would soon proceed now that the Bulette crisis had been resolved.

### Eltharion’s Findings

Meanwhile, Clio joined Eltharion and Ilyra in the Archive.

Eltharion presented the pattern he believes exists across Thurandil’s expansionist campaigns: repeated atrocities, deliberately obscured records, and the recurring involvement of the **Tam Lin**.

The archival work concluded when Clio used magic to put away the large quantity of material Eltharion had removed. The method horrified Ilyra.

That evening, Clio briefed the rest of the party on:

- Eltharion’s discoveries concerning Thurandil
- the identity and role of the Tam Lin
- his suspicion that further answers may be found in the **Glasslands**

The Tam Lin are therefore no longer merely DM-side information. The entire party now knows the name and understands them as Thurandil’s extraordinarily dangerous agents.

When the party asked Garr about No-Name, his demeanor changed immediately.

Garr explained that the dwarves interpreted No-Name’s mushroom cavern as an infection spreading through their ancestral city. They razed the cavern in an effort to halt what they believed was corruption.

Through the burning spore network, Garr heard fragments of No-Name’s pain and rage. He subsequently located the surviving Myconid and concealed him here.

Because Needle was the only party member who ate one of No-Name’s mushrooms during their original meeting, he remains the only person capable of directly receiving the Myconid’s communication.

Needle experienced:

- No-Name’s rage
- the destruction of his home
- the loss of his fungal community
- his sense of betrayal and displacement

The party reinforced the hiding place and began planning how to remove No-Name safely from Farhaven.
